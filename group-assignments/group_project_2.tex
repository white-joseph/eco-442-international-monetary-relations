\section*{Data}

We analyze the Japanese yen relative to the U.S. dollar and quote the exchange rate as
\[
S_0 \equiv E_{\text{JPY}/\$} = \text{yen per 1 U.S. dollar}.
\]

We use the spot and forward quotes:
\[
S_0 = 152.904 \ \text{JPY}/\$.
\]

The interest rates are annualized government yields with matching maturities in each country (3-month and 1-year):

\begin{itemize}
  \item U.S.\ 3-month interest rate: $i_{\$}^{3\text{mo}} = 3.69\%$
  \item Japan 3-month interest rate: $i_{\text{JPY}}^{3\text{mo}} = 0.72\%$
  \item U.S.\ 1-year interest rate: $i_{\$}^{1\text{yr}} = 3.44\%$
  \item Japan 1-year interest rate: $i_{\text{JPY}}^{1\text{yr}} = 1.05\%$
\end{itemize}

\section*{Interest Rate Parity}

Covered interest rate parity:
\[
\frac{1+i_{\$}}{1+i_F} = \frac{F^{\$}_{F}}{E^{\$}_{F}}.
\]

The forward rate is:
\[
F^{\text{IRP}}
=
S_0\left(\frac{1+i_{\text{JPY}}}{1+i_{\$}}\right).
\]

Uncovered interest rate parity is:
\[
\frac{1+i_{\$}}{1+i_F} = \frac{E^{1\text{yr}}{}^{\$}_{F}}{E^{\$}_{F}}.
\]

\section*{(1) IRP Forecasts}

\subsection*{3-month implied forward rate}
\[
F_{3\text{mo}}^{\text{IRP}}
=
152.904\left(\frac{1+0.0072}{1+0.0369}\right)
=
148.519\ \text{JPY}/\$ \approx 148.52.
\]

\subsection*{1-year implied forward rate}
\[
F_{1\text{yr}}^{\text{IRP}}
=
152.904\left(\frac{1+0.0105}{1+0.0344}\right)
=
149.371\ \text{JPY}/\$ \approx 149.37.
\]

\section*{(2) Market Forward Rates and Comparison}

From the USD/JPY forward-rates from FXEmpire we use:
\[
F_{3\text{mo}}^{\text{mkt}} = 151.758, 
\qquad
F_{1\text{yr}}^{\text{mkt}} = 148.998.
\]

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lccc}
\toprule
Horizon & IRP forward $F^{\text{IRP}}$ & Market forward $F^{\text{mkt}}$ & Difference \\
\midrule
3 months & 148.519 & 151.758 & $-3.239$ \\
1 year   & 149.371 & 148.998 & $0.373$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\section*{Discussion}

Since U.S.\ interest rates exceed Japanese interest rates at both maturities ($i_{\$} > i_{\text{JPY}}$),
Covered IRP implies that the yen should trade at a forward premium relative to the dollar, which  means the forward rate should be below the spot rate. This is what we observe for the
1-year forward rate.

Small differences between the IRP-implied forward and observed market forward rates can arise from bid-ask spreads, timing mismatches across sources, and differences in quote/compounding conventions for interest rates and forward contracts.
